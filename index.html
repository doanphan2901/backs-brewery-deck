Executive summary

Back’s Brewery – Supply Chain Network Redesign: Results of Assignments 1–3

Key message: Closing five warehouses increases profit by 59.6%, and the acquisition of Holstan could further improve profit by 27.5%, mainly due to lower production costs. But once seasonal demand is taken into account, the seven-warehouse network no longer has sufficient capacity: even with 11 warehouses, only 36 barrels of spare capacity remain at the 2028 demand peak.

Key findings

1a

Baseline €1.084m (14.8% margin): all 12 warehouses serve all demand, but four run far below capacity – Napoli idle (€133k fixed cost).

1b

Seven warehouses maximise profit: €646k gained (+59.6%) through fixed-cost savings, but spare capacity falls by 94.5% to 1,670 barrels.

1c

400 km rule is infeasible: a 3,632-barrel local capacity gap for Paris, London and Warsaw.

2a

All demand fulfilled: €1.745m profit; plants 84.3% vs. warehouses 98.3% utilised – warehouse capacity limits growth.

2b

Holstan acquisition: profit +27.5% (+€479k) from lower production costs, while transport costs rise by 54.5%.

3a

Seasonal peaks: July peak +14.5% vs. annual demand +4.4%; 7 of 24 months exceed the 2b-site capacity.

3b

Multi-period network: 11 warehouses, delivery distance −23%; profit −23.1% (93% start-up); only 36 barrels spare in July 2028.

Our recommendation

① Consolidate to 7 warehouses

Fixed-cost savings drive the gain, but evaluate service and capacity risks before implementation. If 400 km is mandatory, compare expansion and 3PL on total cost.

② Holstan – with conditions

The actual acquisition and integration costs need to be considered: proceed to due diligence and validate distribution resilience before approval.

③ Plan for the peak, not the average

Do not decide on cost savings alone: future demand, warehouse capacity and customer service matter. Test flexible peak-period capacity or 3PL first.
